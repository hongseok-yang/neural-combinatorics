\section{Purpose and scope}

For a finite graph $H$, define
\[
 \Gamma_H(W):=t(H,W)-\Phi_H(p(W)),
 \qquad
 \Phi_H(p):=(1-p)^{v(H)}
 \chi_H\!\left(\frac1{1-p}\right).
\]
This note develops two systematic negative tests and no others:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item the complete first variation and part-size Hessian, together with the
 complete measurable-kernel Hessian on each critical face of a balanced
 Tur\'an graphon and the coupled part-size/cell expansion through the first
 nonzero coefficient on each flat quadratic ray, plus the complete
 first-order small-support one-microclass test at the threshold and first
 interior Tur\'an grids, its exact Boolean threshold expansion through the
 first nonzero order, and the exact continuum zero set and full
 second-order mass cone at the first interior grid, together with the full
 second-order interaction of arbitrary mixtures of coarse continuum-flat
 threshold profiles and an audit of the extra fibre-overlap moments needed
 beyond the coarse setting;
 \item the exact high-density comparison between the balanced complete
 $k$-partite graphon and the constant graphon, together with its
 cycle-rank interpretation.
\end{enumerate}
The calculations apply to the $43$ catalogue rows that remained open after
the positive page-rooted-book theorem and before the present tests.  Every
final counterexample below is checked by an exact finite sum and rational
arithmetic.  No tensor search is used, and no accepted sign claim relies on
numerical optimization.  The constant-graphon comparison is retained on the
29-row pre-Atlas-152 snapshot, whose other $28$ rows are the current open
set.  The higher-order needle audit retains the preceding $32$-row snapshot,
including the now-positive Atlas $120$, $123$, and $142$ and the now-negative
Atlas $152$, so its aggregate certificate counts remain reproducible.

Throughout, $n=v(H)$, $m=e(H)$, and
\[
 \chi_H(x)=\sum_{j=0}^n a_jx^j.
\]

\section{The full coarse-cell first variation at $T_k$}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

Partition the probability space into $k$ equal classes
$\Omega_1,\ldots,\Omega_k$.  The balanced Tur\'an graphon $T_k$ is zero
on every diagonal cell $\Omega_i^2$ and one on every off-diagonal cell.
Consequently a feasible one-sided perturbation adds a nonnegative kernel on
a diagonal cell or deletes a nonnegative kernel from an off-diagonal cell.

Set
\[
 J_H(k):=\sum_{j=0}^n a_j(j-n)k^j
\]
and, with every edge contraction simplified to an ordinary simple graph,
\[
 A_H(k):=\sum_{e\in E(H)}\chi_{H/e}(k)-J_H(k),
\]
\[
 B_H(k):=2J_H(k)-\frac{2m\chi_H(k)}{k-1}.
\]
The expression defining $B_H$ is a polynomial because
$k(k-1)$ divides $\chi_H(k)$.

\begin{proposition}[Exact cell derivatives]\label{prop:first}
At $T_k$, the derivative of $\Gamma_H$ obtained by adding unit weight on
one diagonal cell is
\[
 \frac{A_H(k)}{k^{n+1}}.
\]
The derivative obtained by deleting unit weight on one unordered
off-diagonal cell is
\[
 \frac{B_H(k)}{k^{n+1}}.
\]
These two numbers exhaust the coarse $k\times k$ cell-value first
variation, by permutation symmetry of the classes.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Differentiate $t(H,W)$ with respect to one cell value.  On a diagonal cell,
the differentiated edge has both ends in one prescribed colour.  After
contracting that edge, the number of compatible colourings is
$\chi_{H/e}(k)/k$.  Summing over the differentiated edge and multiplying by
the assignment probability $k^{-n}$ gives
\[
 \frac1{k^{n+1}}\sum_{e\in E(H)}\chi_{H/e}(k).
\]
On one unordered off-diagonal cell, both orientations of two fixed distinct
colours occur.  Colour symmetry gives
\[
 \frac{2m\chi_H(k)}{k^{n+1}(k-1)}
\]
for the derivative of $t(H,W)$ with respect to increasing that cell.

Writing $q=1-p$, direct differentiation of
$\Phi_H(p)=\sum_j a_jq^{n-j}$ gives
\[
 \Phi_H'\!\left(1-\frac1k\right)
 =\frac{J_H(k)}{k^{n-1}}.
\]
A diagonal cell has measure $k^{-2}$ and an unordered off-diagonal cell has
measure $2k^{-2}$.  Subtracting the derivative of the target, and reversing
the sign for an off-diagonal deletion, gives the displayed formulas.
Every coarse cell belongs to one of the two orbits under $S_k$.
\end{proof}

If $k\geq\chi(H)$, then $T_k$ is an interior point of the required density
interval, so first-order stability requires $A_H(k),B_H(k)\geq0$.  At the
threshold $k=\chi(H)-1$, let $D$ be the total diagonal addition amplitude
and $O$ the total off-diagonal deletion amplitude.  Feasibility of the
density constraint is
\[
 D-2O\geq0.
\]
Hence first-order stability at the threshold is equivalent to
\begin{equation}
 A_H(k)\geq0,
 \qquad
 2A_H(k)+B_H(k)\geq0.
 \label{eq:threshold-kkt}
\end{equation}
This is the exact tangent-cone, or KKT, test; no direction has been selected
in advance.

For every one of the $43$ tested rows, the coefficients of
\[
 A_H(y+\chi(H)),\qquad B_H(y+\chi(H))
\]
are nonnegative.  Thus every interior Tur\'an point $T_k$ passes the complete
coarse-cell first-order test for every integer $k\geq\chi(H)$.  On the
pre-Atlas-152 snapshot of $29$ rows, the two values are in fact strict at
$k=\chi(H)$, and hence at every larger integer.  The independent note
\path{notes/turan_arbitrary_linfty_local_stability.tex} combines this strict
functional with a uniform multilinear Taylor bound.  It proves that every
$T_k$, $k\geq\chi(H)$, is a strict local minimizer against completely
arbitrary measurable $L^\infty$-small cell kernels, and gives an explicit
rational neighbourhood radius.  This upgrades the formal first variation
to a genuine graphon-local theorem; it does not assume that the perturbation
is constant on any cell.

The same Taylor argument applies at the critical threshold whenever both
tangent-cone inequalities are strict.  Writing $r=\chi(H)-1$, it gives the
exact slope
\[
 \frac1{r^{v(H)-1}}
 \min\left\{A_H(r),\frac{2A_H(r)+B_H(r)}4\right\}
\]
and hence an explicit arbitrary-kernel $L^\infty$ radius.  Among the snapshot
rows this settles the complete threshold neighbourhood for the following
$14$ rows, all of which remain current:
\[
126,127,147,151,157,160,168,169,178,181,185,194,196,199.
\]
The full exact table is in
\path{notes/turan_arbitrary_linfty_local_stability.tex}.  The other $15$
snapshot rows have a genuine first-order critical face and are not claimed by
this strict theorem; one of them, Atlas $152$, is now negative.

The same note now closes five of the $14$ current critical rows by a second,
coercive argument.  Atlas $43,118,122,124$, and $203$ have
$A_H(r)=0<B_H(r)$, so only diagonal addition is first-order flat.  Splitting
an arbitrary perturbation as $U=F-V$ on diagonal and off-diagonal cells, the

\[
 Q_H(F)\geq q_HS,
 \qquad

\]
Every pure-$F$ term of order at least three is bounded by $K_D\eta S$, while
every nonlinear term containing $V$ is bounded by

gives explicit arbitrary-measurable $L^\infty$ radii from $1/14251389$ to

The remaining nine critical rows are now closed by two sign-preserving

$153,171,174,188$ the exact target factors as

Thus every one of the $28$ current open rows now has a complete

\section{The complete part-size Hessian mode}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

Cell-value perturbations keep the class measures fixed.  We next test all
infinitesimal imbalances of the part measures.  By $S_k$ symmetry, the
zero-sum part-size space is one irreducible standard representation, so it
has a single Hessian eigenvalue.  It is therefore enough, and exhaustive, to
replace two masses by
\[
 \frac1k+\varepsilon,\qquad \frac1k-\varepsilon
\]
and leave the other $k-2$ masses equal to $1/k$.

For a proper $k$-colouring $c$, let $n_i(c)$ denote the size of colour class
$i$.  Define
\[
 S_H(k):=
 \sum_{c\text{ proper}}
 \left[
  \binom{n_1(c)}2+\binom{n_2(c)}2-n_1(c)n_2(c)
 \right].
\]
This is an exact polynomial in $k$.  A form convenient for computation and
independent review is
\[
 S_H(k)
 =2\sum_{\{u,v\}\notin E(H)}\frac{\chi_{H/uv}(k)}k
 -2\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq V(H)}
      \frac{\chi_{H+uv}(k)}{k(k-1)}.
\]
Put
\[
 C_H(k):=kS_H(k)+2J_H(k).
\]

\begin{proposition}[Part-size eigenvalue]\label{prop:part-size}
For the part-size perturbation above,
\[
 \Gamma_H(W_\varepsilon)
 =\frac{C_H(k)}{k^{n-1}}\varepsilon^2+O(\varepsilon^3).
\]
For all $43$ tested rows and every real $k\geq\chi(H)$, this coefficient is
strictly positive.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For a fixed proper colouring, expand
\[
 \left(\frac1k+\varepsilon\right)^{n_1}
 \left(\frac1k-\varepsilon\right)^{n_2}
 \left(\frac1k\right)^{n-n_1-n_2}.
\]
Its quadratic coefficient is $k^{2-n}$ times the summand defining $S_H(k)$.
Also
\[
 p(W_\varepsilon)=1-\sum_i\mu(\Omega_i)^2
 =1-\frac1k-2\varepsilon^2.
\]
Using the formula for $\Phi_H'$ in the proof of
Proposition~\ref{prop:first}, the quadratic gap coefficient is
\[
 k^{2-n}S_H(k)+2\frac{J_H(k)}{k^{n-1}}
 =\frac{C_H(k)}{k^{n-1}}.
\]

For each tested graph, exact polynomial arithmetic shows that every
coefficient of $C_H(y+\chi(H))$ is nonnegative and its constant coefficient
is positive.  Therefore $C_H(k)>0$ throughout the asserted range.
\end{proof}

Thus unequal complete-partite blow-ups do not yield a local negative case
among these rows.

\section{Coupling part sizes and cell values}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The preceding two calculations must not simply be read separately.  A part
imbalance and a cell perturbation may occur at the same time, and their mixed
second derivative can have either sign.  We now include that term exactly.
For $T_2$, write
\[
 \mu(A)=\frac12+\varepsilon z,\qquad
 \mu(B)=\frac12-\varepsilon z
\]
and put $\varepsilon F,\varepsilon K,1-\varepsilon G$ on
$A^2,B^2,A\times B$, respectively, where $a,b,g$ are the three conditional
cell means.  Direct expansion of the finite macro-colouring sum gives
\begin{equation}
 p(W_\varepsilon)
 =\frac12+\frac{a+b-2g}{4}\varepsilon
  +z(a-b-2z)\varepsilon^2+O(\varepsilon^3).
 \label{eq:coupled-density}
\end{equation}

We use the following names for the three critical first-order faces:
type I means $A_H(2)=0<B_H(2)$ and hence $g=0$; type II means
$A_H(2)=B_H(2)=0$; and type III means
$A_H(2)>0$, $2A_H(2)+B_H(2)=0$, and hence $a+b=2g$.
On a type-I critical face, $g=0$, and the four mean-only quadratic
certificates become
\[
\begin{array}{c|l}
43&32Q=a^2+b^2-4(a-b)z+16z^2\\
118,122&64Q=a^2+b^2-4(a-b)z+16z^2\\
124&32Q=a^2-ab+b^2-4(a-b)z+8z^2.
\end{array}
\]
They are positive definite; the omitted rooted-degree variance terms are
nonnegative.  On every type-II face, the formulas in the preceding table are
unchanged: the mixed $z$-terms cancel.  On a type-III face, where $b=2g-a$,
they acquire the following terms:
\[
\begin{array}{c|l}
153&32Q=3ab+2g^2+8z(a-g-z)\\
171&16Q=ab+4z(a-g-z)\\
174&32Q=3ab+8z(a-g-z)\\
188&32Q=ab+8z(a-g-z).
\end{array}
\]
By \eqref{eq:coupled-density}, feasibility through second order requires
$z(a-g-z)\geq0$.  Thus all four expressions remain nonnegative.

There are two higher-order boundary issues.  They can also be closed for
arbitrary bounded measurable cell kernels, rather than only for constant
coarse cells.  We first record the elementary tree inequality needed for
the only nonzero type-II flat ray.

\begin{lemma}[Three-edge path inequality]\label{lem:path-three}
If $F$ is a bounded symmetric nonnegative kernel on a probability space and
$a=\int F$, then
\[
 t(P_4,F)\geq a^3,
\]
where $P_4$ denotes the path with three edges.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
This is the three-edge instance of the tree homomorphism inequality.  For
completeness, the standard entropy proof works for every finite tree $T$.
After replacing $F$ by $F+\delta$ and passing to the limit, assume that its
degree function $d$ is positive almost everywhere.  Root $T$, give the root
density $d/a$, and, independently down the oriented tree, use transition
density $F(x,y)/d(x)$.  The resulting probability density is
\[
 P_T(\boldsymbol{x})
 =\frac1a\prod_{uv\in E(T)}F(x_u,x_v)
   \prod_{v\in V(T)}d(x_v)^{1-\deg_T(v)}.
\]
Every vertex marginal is $d/a$.  Comparing $P_T$ by relative entropy with
the probability density
$\prod_{uv\in E(T)}F(x_u,x_v)/t(T,F)$ gives
\[
 \log t(T,F)
 \geq \log a+\bigl(e(T)-1\bigr)
       \frac1a\int d\log d.
\]
Convexity of $s\mapsto s\log s$ gives
$a^{-1}\int d\log d\geq\log a$, and hence
$t(T,F)\geq a^{e(T)}$.  Taking $T=P_4$ proves the claim.  The
$\delta\downarrow0$ passage follows by monotone convergence.
\end{proof}

For Atlas $152$, every nonzero Hessian-flat type-II direction has, up to
exchanging the two parts, $K=G=0$.  Let $F$ be the remaining diagonal
kernel, let $a=\int F$, and allow an arbitrary simultaneous part displacement
$z$.  Exact macro-colouring enumeration shows that the quadratic selected
edges form $2K_2$ in exactly two colourings, while the cubic selected edges
form $P_4$ in exactly four colourings.  Consequently its full cubic gap is
\[
 \frac{4t(P_4,F)-a^3-8a^2z+32az^2}{64}.
\]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:path-three}, this is at least
\[
 \frac{a}{64}
 \left(32\left(z-\frac a8\right)^2+\frac52a^2\right)>0
 \qquad(a>0).
\]
Thus the cubic conclusion for Atlas $152$ is not a regular-kernel
assumption.

For the type-III quadratic zero rays of Atlas $171,174,188$, exchange the
parts if necessary so that $a=0$, $b=2g$.  Feasibility and vanishing of the
quadratic coefficient leave exactly $z=0$ and $z=-g$.  At $z=0$, the edge
density is identically $1/2$, so the target is zero and the homomorphism
density is nonnegative.  At $z=-g$, exact mean calculation gives
\[
 p(W_\varepsilon)=\frac12+4g^3\varepsilon^3.
\]
For each of these three graphs,
\[
 \Phi_H'(1/2)=-\frac18.
\]
The full first and second coefficients of $t(H,W_\varepsilon)$ vanish on
this flat ray.  Since that density is nonnegative for all sufficiently small
$\varepsilon\geq0$, its first possible, cubic coefficient is nonnegative.
The cubic gap is therefore at least $g^3/2>0$ when $g>0$.  This proves the
arbitrary-kernel endpoint statement without replacing $K$ or $G$ by their
means.  The displayed constant-cell cubic pairs
\[
 (1/2,1)g^3,\qquad(3/4,5/4)g^3,\qquad(1/4,3/4)g^3
\]
remain useful exact checks, but are no longer the justification for the
general conclusion.

Second, a pure part imbalance has no first-order cell direction and lowers
the density by $2z^2\varepsilon^2$.  Normalize $z=1$ and repair it using
arbitrary bounded diagonal kernels of means
$c_0\varepsilon^2,c_1\varepsilon^2$ and an arbitrary bounded cross-deletion
kernel of mean $h\varepsilon^2$.  Feasibility requires
\[
 c_0+c_1-2h\geq8.
\]
For every type-II face other than the two already negative rows, exact
quartic expansion followed by $c_0c_1\leq(c_0+c_1)^2/4$ or
$c_0c_1\geq0$ gives a strictly positive polynomial after writing
$c_0+c_1=2h+8+\delta$, $\delta\geq0$.  For reference, the certified
type-II lower numerators (with denominators respectively
$64,32,32,32,32,64,32$) are
\[
\begin{array}{c|l}

130&\delta^2+8\delta h+32\delta+8h^2+64h+128\\
137,139&\delta^2+4\delta h+16\delta+4h^2+32h+64\\
145&\frac34\delta^2+7\delta h+28\delta+7h^2+56h+112\\
148&\frac12\delta^2+10\delta h+40\delta+10h^2+80h+160\\
152&4\delta h+16\delta+4h^2+32h+64.
\end{array}
\]
On a type-III face, equality in the linear KKT condition forces the same
displayed equality.  The cubic density displacement is proportional to
$c_0-c_1$, and feasibility forces it to be nonnegative; the gap has the
same-sign cubic term $(c_0-c_1)/8$.  When this also vanishes,
$c_0=c_1=h+4$, and the four quartic coefficients for Atlas

\[
 \frac{5h^2+56h+112}{32},\quad
 \frac{h^2+16h+32}{16},\quad
 \frac{3h^2+40h+80}{32},\quad
 \frac{h^2+24h+48}{32},
\]
all strictly positive.

The displayed quartic lower bounds remain valid for these arbitrary repair
kernels.  Indeed, two repair edges contribute exactly the full
measurable-kernel Hessian from the next section, while every term involving
the part displacement and at most one repair edge depends only on the three
cell means.  Relative to the constant-cell expressions above, the only

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}

\end{array}
\]
where $u,v$ are rooted-degree variances and hence nonnegative.  On the
type-III critical face all rooted-moment terms cancel exactly, as shown by
the full Hessian table below.  Therefore the density-repaired quartic checks
also cover arbitrary bounded measurable repair kernels.

At the three-part faces, minimize the same joint calculation exactly over
the two free part-size displacements.  For Atlas $203$ at the threshold, the
residual mean-only quadratic form is
\[
 \frac{35\sum_i d_i^2+57\sum_{i<j}d_id_j}{2916}>0
\]
unless every $d_i$ vanishes.  Atlas $172$ at the interior point $T_3$ is the

Thus coupling the symmetry-complete part-size mode to all critical cell

valid for arbitrary bounded measurable kernels.  This remains a
finite-order statement for perturbations represented relative to the fixed
Tur\'an partition; it is not a proof of local minimality in cut distance or
in any other topology on the full graphon quotient space.


%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{The complete measurable-kernel threshold Hessian}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

When the first variation vanishes, a coarse constant-cell test can miss
inhomogeneous modes inside a Tur\'an class.  We now compute the full Hessian
for arbitrary measurable perturbation kernels, rather than selecting a
finite-step direction.

First let $\chi(H)=3$ and work at $T_2$.  Regard each of its two parts as a
copy of an arbitrary probability space.  Every feasible first-order
perturbation has the form
\begin{equation}
 W_\varepsilon|_{A^2}=\varepsilon F,\qquad
 W_\varepsilon|_{B^2}=\varepsilon K,\qquad
 W_\varepsilon|_{A\times B}=1-\varepsilon G,
 \label{eq:full-perturbation}
\end{equation}
where $F,K,G\geq0$ are bounded measurable kernels, $F$ and $K$ are
symmetric, and the cross-cell formula is transposed on $B\times A$.
Write
\[
 a=\int F,\qquad b=\int K,\qquad g=\int G,
 \qquad s=a+b.
\]
The threshold constraint is exactly
\begin{equation}
 s\geq2g.
 \label{eq:hessian-feasible}
\end{equation}
If $f=T_F\mathbf1$ and $k=T_K\mathbf1$, put
\[
 u=\int f^2-a^2\geq0,
 \qquad
 v=\int k^2-b^2\geq0.
\]

The coefficient of $\varepsilon^2$ can be computed without any step
approximation.  Choose the two differentiated edges of $H$.  If they are
disjoint, their integral is a product of cell means.  If they share a
vertex, their integral is an inner product of the appropriate rooted degree

 \int f g_A,\quad\int k g_B,

\section{Small-support, order-one needle perturbations}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

The preceding derivatives scale the value change on each fixed Tur\'an cell
by $\varepsilon$.  Smallness in $L^1$ or cut distance permits a different
regime: change edge values by order one on a vertex set of measure
$\varepsilon$.  Several changed edges incident with the same exceptional
vertex then contribute already at order $\varepsilon$, so this regime is not
contained in the ordinary edge-by-edge first variation.  We now test it
without choosing a connection direction in advance.

Fix $k\geq2$.  Split a set $S_\varepsilon$ of measure $\varepsilon$ from
part zero of $T_k$.  For $x\in S_\varepsilon$, let
$w_i(x)\in[0,1]$ be the conditional mean of its connections to the bulk of
part $i$.  Connections inside $S_\varepsilon$ affect only terms of order
$\varepsilon^2$.  Put
\[
 D(\boldsymbol w):=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}w_i-(k-1).
\]
For a vertex $v\in V(H)$ and a proper $k$-colouring $c$ of $H-v$, define
\[
 R_{v,k}(\boldsymbol w)
 :=\sum_{c:H-v\longrightarrow[k]\ {m proper}}
   \prod_{u\in N_H(v)}w_{c(u)},
 \qquad
 R_{H,k}:=\sum_{v\in V(H)}R_{v,k}.
\]
Finally, with $J_H$ as in Section~2, set
\begin{equation}
 Q_{H,k}(\boldsymbol w)
 :=kR_{H,k}(\boldsymbol w)-n\chi_H(k)
   -2J_H(k)D(\boldsymbol w).
 \label{eq:needle-Q}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Exact needle coefficient]\label{prop:needle}
For the perturbation above,
\[
 p(W_\varepsilon)
 =1-\frac1k+\frac{2\varepsilon}{k}
   \int_{S_\varepsilon}D(\boldsymbol w(x))
       \frac{d\mu(x)}{\varepsilon}
   +O(\varepsilon^2),
\]
and
\[
 \Gamma_H(W_\varepsilon)
 =\frac{\varepsilon}{k^n}
   \int_{S_\varepsilon}Q_{H,k}(\boldsymbol w(x))
       \frac{d\mu(x)}{\varepsilon}
   +O(\varepsilon^2).
\]
These formulas allow arbitrary measurable connections from the exceptional
set to the bulk; only their $k$ conditional means enter at first order.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The ordered pairs with exactly one endpoint in $S_\varepsilon$ give the
displayed density coefficient.  For the $H$-density, first consider maps
with no vertex in $S_\varepsilon$.  Decreasing the mass of bulk part zero by
$\varepsilon$ contributes
\[
 -\frac{n\chi_H(k)}{k^n}.
\]
If exactly $v$ maps into $S_\varepsilon$, the other vertices must give a
proper colouring of $H-v$.  For any one such colouring, all surviving bulk
edges have value one.  The variables assigned to a common colour class are
independent and that class is an independent set, so integration of the
edges from the exceptional point gives precisely
$\prod_{u\in N_H(v)}w_{c(u)}$.  Summing over $v$ and $c$ gives
$k^{1-n}R_{H,k}$.  Maps using at least two exceptional vertices have measure
$O(\varepsilon^2)$.

The derivative of the target at $p=1-1/k$ is
$J_H(k)/k^{n-1}$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:first}.  Multiplying it by the
edge-density coefficient and subtracting proves
\eqref{eq:needle-Q}.  The same calculation applies pointwise in the
exceptional variable and then integrates, proving the measurable-kernel
statement.
\end{proof}

At the threshold $k=\chi(H)-1$, abbreviate $A=A_H(k)$.  If the averaged
density direction $\overline D$ is negative, it can be repaired to first
order by adding uniform weight
$-2k\overline D\,\varepsilon$ on one diagonal bulk cell.  By
Proposition~\ref{prop:first}, the resulting addition to the scaled gap
coefficient is $-2A\overline D$.  Hence either of the pointwise certificates
\begin{equation}
 Q_{H,k}(\boldsymbol w)\geq0
 \quad\hbox{or}\quad
 Q_{H,k}(\boldsymbol w)-2A_H(k)D(\boldsymbol w)\geq0
 \qquad(\boldsymbol w\in[0,1]^k)
 \label{eq:needle-threshold-supports}
\end{equation}
proves nonnegativity after averaging and after the necessary density repair.
Indeed, the first certificate has multiplier zero and the second has
multiplier $2A$; these are the two endpoints of the relevant KKT multiplier
interval.  This use of a single global multiplier is essential: separate
certificates on $D\geq0$ and $D\leq0$ would not justify mixing heterogeneous
profiles.

The certificates in this section are exact coefficient certificates.  On a
box, write a polynomial in the tensor-product Bernstein basis of its exact
coordinatewise degrees.  Nonnegative Bernstein coefficients prove
nonnegativity on the whole box.  For the first interior point
$k=\chi(H)$, the polynomial $Q_{H,k}$ is symmetric in all $w_i$.  It is
therefore enough to use the ordered simplex
\[
 0\leq w_0\leq w_1\leq\cdots\leq w_{k-1}\leq1,
\]
whose vertices have respectively $0,1,\ldots,k$ trailing coordinates equal
to one.  Pull back to barycentric coordinates
$\lambda_0,\ldots,\lambda_k$, homogenize, and multiply by
$(\lambda_0+\cdots+\lambda_k)^N$.  This multiplication does not change the
polynomial on the simplex.  Nonnegative monomial coefficients of the
resulting homogeneous polynomial give an exact rational certificate.

\begin{proposition}[Needle no-hit certificates]\label{prop:needle-no-hit}
Every row in the $32$-row open snapshot preceding the two triangle-book
two-edge-tail theorems passes the complete first-order
one-microclass needle test at both
\[
 T_{\chi(H)-1}\quad\hbox{and}\quad T_{\chi(H)}.
\]
At the threshold, the direct certificate in
\eqref{eq:needle-threshold-supports} works alone for Atlas
$126,147,168$, the density-repaired certificate works alone for Atlas
$153,171,174,188$, and both work for the other $25$ rows.
At $T_{\chi(H)}$, the required barycentric degree elevations are
\[
\begin{array}{c|l}
N&\text{Atlas IDs}\\ \hline
1&139,142,171\\
2&120,123,130,137,153,157,160,169,178,181,194,196\\
3&127,185,203\\
4&145,147,148,188\\
5&152,174,199\\
8&43\\
9&168\\
10&126\\
11&122,124,151\\
12&118.
\end{array}
\]
All coefficients referred to here are exact nonnegative rationals.
\end{proposition}

The script reconstructs every polynomial from proper colourings and checks
every displayed coefficient.  As a check that this test is capable of
detecting a genuine instability, it rediscovers Atlas $206$ at its threshold
$T_3$: the exact profile
$(w_0,w_1,w_2)=(1/2,1,1)$ has $D=1/2$ and

Proposition~\ref{prop:needle-no-hit} is strictly a first-order statement in
the exceptional mass.  It does not analyze a profile on which the certified

distance and does not classify any additional row as positive.

\section{Higher-order Boolean boundary needles}

The zero directions left by Proposition~\ref{prop:needle-no-hit} require
rooted two-vertex rather than rooted one-vertex data.  We make two exact
extensions.  They remain local tests around balanced complete multipartite
graphons and choose no graph-specific rewiring direction.

First consider the threshold $k=\chi(H)-1$.  Restrict the exceptional
connection profile to a cube vertex
\[
 \boldsymbol w\in\{0,1\}^k
\]
and let $r\in\{0,1\}$ be the value on the exceptional diagonal cell.  Give
the exceptional class mass $\varepsilon$, reduce the mass of bulk part zero
from $1/k$ to $1/k-\varepsilon$, and retain the remaining Tur\'an cells.
Every Boolean profile and both extreme internal values are tested.

If the resulting exact edge density $p_{\varepsilon}$ leaves the admissible
interval, use an unchanged bulk part of mass $1/k$ and put the exact diagonal
weight
\begin{equation}
 y_{\varepsilon}
 :=k^2\left(\frac{k-1}{k}-p_{\varepsilon}\right)
 \label{eq:boolean-exact-repair}
\end{equation}
on that part.  Whenever repair is invoked, the first nonzero coefficient of
$y_{\varepsilon}$ is positive.  Thus $0\leq y_{\varepsilon}\leq1$ for all
sufficiently small positive $\varepsilon$, and
\eqref{eq:boolean-exact-repair} restores the threshold density exactly, not
merely through a truncated order.

For each Boolean profile on which the admissible first-order coefficient is
zero, the script reconstructs the full polynomial
\[
 \Gamma_H(W_{\varepsilon})
 =t(H,W_{\varepsilon})-\Phi_H(p(W_{\varepsilon})).
\]
There are $174$ choices after including $r=0,1$.  Of these, $106$ require
the exact density repair.  The first-nonzero-order distribution is
\[

\end{array}
\]
Every displayed first nonzero coefficient is strictly positive.  Hence no
threshold Boolean boundary profile produces a sufficiently small local
counterexample.  The identically zero cases are harmless refinements or
relabelings of the Tur\'an construction.

The Boolean audit can be extended over every continuum-flat one-profile
threshold face.  The nonnegative tensor-product Bernstein supports show that
only six rows have a supporting polynomial vanishing on a positive-dimensional
region.  For Atlas $130,152$, the direct coefficient is identically zero and
the admissible flat region is

 \qquad w_0+w_1\geq1.
\]
For Atlas $153,171,174,188$, the repaired coefficient is identically zero and
the flat region is the opposite triangle $w_0+w_1\leq1$.  Every other
supporting zero face either is finite or lies in the wrong density branch and
reduces to an endpoint after the required repair.

For each of the six rows and both $r=0,1$, reconstruct the exact coefficient
$C_{H,r}(w_0,w_1)$ of $\varepsilon^2$.  On the first two rows no repair is
used.  On the other four, equation~\eqref{eq:boolean-exact-repair} is imposed
as a polynomial identity before extracting the coefficient.  Pulling each
$C_{H,r}$ back to its triangle gives nonnegative homogeneous barycentric
coefficients with degree elevation zero.  Thus all $12$ exact triangle

\]
throughout the appropriate continuum-flat region.  Affinity in the internal
cell value extends the conclusion from $r=0,1$ to every $r\in[0,1]$.  No
one-profile continuum threshold direction is negative at second order.

This one-profile statement can be upgraded without selecting finitely many

 \qquad

\]
For Atlas $130,152$ use $\Delta_+$ with no repair; for Atlas

unchanged bulk diagonal cell.  If two exceptional points have profiles

two-root enumeration gives a symmetric mixed quadratic kernel
$K_{H,r}(z,z')$.  Its diagonal is exactly the one-profile quadratic
coefficient already certified above.

The coarse restriction can be removed completely for five of the six rows.
For arbitrary measurable exceptional-to-bulk fibres, a two-root term also

\[
 h_0(z,z')=\int_A f_zf_{z'},
 \qquad

\]
not only on the means $u,v,u',v'$.  These satisfy the sharp Fr\'echet bounds
\[
 \max(0,u+u'-1)\leq h_0\leq\min(u,u'),
 \quad
 \max(0,v+v'-1)\leq h_1\leq\min(v,v').
\]
They cannot simply be replaced by $uu'$ and $vv'$.  Nevertheless, for Atlas

itself coefficientwise nonnegative in the four profile means.  The worst
case is therefore the sharp Fr\'echet lower endpoint.  The two switches
partition $\Delta_\pm^2$ into six explicit four-simplices.  For each of the
five rows and $r=0,1$, the pullback to every simplex has nonnegative
homogeneous coefficients without degree elevation.  These $60$ exact
certificates prove pointwise nonnegativity of the full arbitrary-fibre pair
kernel, and hence nonnegativity of the integrated quadratic coefficient.

partition, and coefficient signatures are given independently in

Atlas $152$ is the exact boundary.  For $r=0$, the two flat profiles
\[
 (u,v)=\left(\frac16,1\right),
 \qquad
 (u',v')=\left(\frac56,\frac23\right)
\]
can be realized by indicator fibres with
$h_0=0$ and $h_1=2/3$.  The exact mixed kernel is then
\[
 K_{152,0}(z,z')=-\frac1{324}.
\]
Thus pointwise nonnegativity of the full arbitrary-fibre kernel is false.
This is not a negative mass direction: for these two indicator profiles the
entire two-type matrix is
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}
  5/288&-1/324\\
  -1/324&2/9
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 \det=\frac{101}{26244}>0.
\]
The example precisely separates the five proved arbitrary-fibre cones from
what was then the unresolved integrated Atlas $152$ problem; it is not a counterexample to
the graph inequality.  The seven positive terms of the exact integrated
functional do factor jointly as
\[
 \int \mathcal A(z,z')\mathcal A(z',z)\,dz\,dz',
 \qquad
 \mathcal A(z,z')=v(z)h_0(z,z')+u(z)h_1(z,z')+u(z)v(z).
\]
However, the natural contraction of this quantity by
$(\int\mathcal A)^2$ is false: an exact two-atom fibre example misses it by
$101/46656$, while retaining positive true Atlas $152$ quadratic gap
$1543/11664$.  A stronger-looking geometric-mean contraction of the two
kernel orientations also fails, by $3/32$ on a second exact two-atom fibre
example, while its true quadratic gap remains $9/256>0$.  The full
independent relaxation obtained by minimizing every diagonal Jensen bound
and off-diagonal Frechet bound also has an exact noncopositive two-profile
matrix, but those minima are not jointly realizable by common fibres; the
compatible indicator-fibre matrix is positive definite.  The full
identities, examples, and precise safe lessons are in the companion note
and Failed Attempts~24--27.

Common-fibre consistency in fact settles every mixture supported on at most
two profiles when both fibre families are indicators.  The diagonal entry is
\[
 D(u,v)=\frac{9u^2v^2-4(u+v-1)^2}{8}\geq0.
\]
The mixed entry is overlap-monotone, so its lower envelope occurs at the two
sharp Frechet lower intersections.  On the six resulting four-simplices,
the exact determinant has nonnegative homogeneous coefficients with
signatures
\[
 (8,365,9/64),(8,365,9/64),(8,410,9/64),
 (8,474,9/64),(8,244,21/64),(8,244,21/64).
\]
If the actual mixed entry is negative, monotonicity moves it toward zero and
preserves the determinant bound; if it is nonnegative, copositivity is
immediate.  Hence arbitrary masses, crossing indicator sets, and an arbitrary
exceptional--exceptional kernel are all safe in the two-profile cone.

There is no profile-count restriction on the two axes.  Zero-displacement
profiles may first be deleted because the unpaired joint-kernel integrand is
nonnegative and both sides then scale quadratically.  If every remaining
profile has $u=1$ or $v=1$, the exact same-axis and cross-axis kernels are
\[
 \frac{h(h+2x+2y)}8,
 \qquad
 \frac{xy(xy+2x+2y)}8,
\]
respectively.  Both are pointwise nonnegative.  Thus arbitrary mixtures of
axis profiles, with arbitrary crossings of their unconstrained fibres, are
safe.  A residual indicator construction must contain at least three
crossing profiles and positive mass on the strict interior
$u<1,v<1,u+v>1$.

Arbitrary fractional fibres are also safe when the mean-profile support is a
coordinatewise chain.  For comparable profiles $u\leq u'$, $v\leq v'$, put
\[
 u=1-d-y,\quad v=1-c-x,
 \quad u'=1-d,\quad v'=1-c.
\]
Then $c,d,x,y\geq0$, $c+d+x+y\leq1$, and the two lower overlaps are
$(1-2d-y)_+$ and $(1-2c-x)_+$.  The switch regions triangulate into seven
central four-simplices and two side simplices, with the both-zero region
reduced to one boundary point.  The nine lower-envelope kernels have exact
degree-four coefficientwise nonnegative certificates, with $53$--$68$
nonzero coefficients and least positive coefficient at least $1/128$ except
for two stronger entries.  An exact active-facet audit also checks that all
$36$ simplex pairs meet face-to-face, with shared-vertex incidence counts
$1:3$, $2:9$, $3:13$, and $4:11$.  Hence every comparable pair kernel is
pointwise nonnegative.  No nesting or indicator assumption is placed on the
actual fibres.  A residual construction must therefore give positive product
measure to incomparable mean-profile pairs.

The complete transverse antichain at one density displacement is safe as
well.  If every profile has $u+v-1=t$, orient a pair by $u\leq u'$ and write
\[
 (u,v)=(t+a,1-a),\qquad
 (u',v')=(t+a+b,1-a-b).
\]
The domain $t,a,b\geq0$, $t+a+b\leq1$ is cut by the two lower-overlap
switches into four tetrahedra.  Their lower-envelope kernels have exact
degree-four signatures
\[
 (4,22,1/128),(4,26,1/16),(4,27,1/128),(4,22,1/128).
\]
All six tetrahedron pairs are certified face-to-face by exact rational
active-facet enumeration.
Hence arbitrary fractional and crossing fibres are nonnegative on every
constant-displacement profile line.  Any residual construction must combine
incomparable profiles with genuinely varying density displacement.

There is a complementary arbitrary-fractional theorem with one fixed mean
profile.  If $u(z)=u$ and $v(z)=v$ almost everywhere, the joint kernel is
symmetric.  Jensen and the two Gram identities give
\[
 \mathbb E\mathcal A^2\geq(\mathbb E\mathcal A)^2
 \geq\bigl(uv(u+v+1)\bigr)^2\geq4(u+v-1)^2,
\]
because
\[
 uv(u+v+1)-2(u+v-1)
 =uv(u+v-1)+2(1-u)(1-v)\geq0.
\]
Thus the fibres may be arbitrary fractional functions and cross without
restriction when their mean profile is fixed.  A residual fractional
construction must vary the mean profile.

This has an explicit dispersion extension.  For
$\bar u=\mathbb E u$, $\bar v=\mathbb E v$, and
$\bar d=\bar u+\bar v-1$, put
\[
 \mathcal G(\bar u,\bar v)
 =\bigl(\bar u\bar v(\bar u+\bar v+1)\bigr)^2-4\bar d^2.
\]
Freezing the scalar coefficients in $\mathcal A$ bounds the integrated
product loss by
$12(\mathbb E|u-\bar u|+\mathbb E|v-\bar v|)$.  Therefore the full
arbitrary-fibre quadratic coefficient is nonnegative whenever
\[
 12\bigl(\mathbb E|u-\bar u|+\mathbb E|v-\bar v|\bigr)
 \leq\mathcal G(\bar u,\bar v).
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz gives the simpler sufficient bound
$12(\sigma_u+\sigma_v)\leq\mathcal G$.  The earlier uniform radius also
follows: if both coordinates lie within $\varepsilon$ of their averages, it
is enough that $\varepsilon\leq\mathcal G/24$.  These radii are positive
except at the two zero-displacement vertices $(1,0),(0,1)$.

A tempting strengthening of the joint contraction is also exactly false.
With profile masses $3/5,2/5$, profiles $(9/10,1/10),(1/4,3/4)$, constant
first-bulk fibres $9/10,1/4$, and second-bulk fibres
$(2/5,0,0,0),(0,1,1,1)$ on four equal atoms, the joint matrix is
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}207/1000&9/80\\57/160&27/64\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Writing $C=\mathbb E[uv]$, its integrated product is
$25255881/400000000$, while $C=129/1000$ and the difference from $4C^2$ is
$-1369719/400000000$.  The true density displacement is zero, so this is a
failed proof inequality rather than a negative Atlas direction; it is
recorded in Failed Attempt~27.

The same consistency perspective does settle an infinite-dimensional
subcone.  If the two families of fibres are each comonotone, their overlaps
are $\min\{u,u'\}$ and $\min\{v,v'\}$.  Concavity on the upper profile
triangle reduces the required bound to its three vertices and proves each
orientation of the joint kernel is at least twice its density displacement.
Hence the Atlas $152$ pair kernel is pointwise nonnegative for every mixture
of nested indicator fibres.  Together with the two-profile, axis-profile,
comparable-mean, equal-displacement, and bounded-dispersion theorems, this
showed that any remaining obstruction had to put positive mass on
strict-interior profiles, give positive measure to incomparable profiles
with varying displacement, and use genuinely fractional fibres unless at
least three indicator profiles crossed.

That residual cone contains an exact counterexample.  Take profile masses
$1/10,9/10$, mean profiles
\[
 (624/625,1/16),\qquad(1/625,1),
\]
complementary indicator fibres in the first bulk part, and constant fibres
$1/16,1$ in the second.  The exact eightfold quadratic matrix is
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}

 \end{pmatrix},
\]
with determinant $-2943/312500000000$ and quadratic value
$-8631/2000000000$ on the displayed mass vector.  The companion proof is the
standalone Atlas 152 fractional-fibre note in the \texttt{notes} directory.  It
evaluates the complete five-step graphon at $\varepsilon=1/3000$, where
\[
 p=\frac{18750093281}{37500000000}>\frac12,
\]
and the full gap is strictly negative.  Hence
Atlas $152$ is negative; this conclusion uses neither an asymptotic truncation
nor a tensor construction.

The next extension treats the full zero set at the first interior point
$k=\chi(H)$.  Recall that $Q_{H,k}$ is symmetric.  Pull it back to the
ordered simplex
\[
 0\leq w_0\leq\cdots\leq w_{k-1}\leq1
\]
and use the nonnegative homogeneous barycentric certificate from
Proposition~\ref{prop:needle-no-hit}.  Because every monomial coefficient is
nonnegative, a point is a zero precisely when every positive monomial
vanishes there.  The exact support audit gives only the barycentric vertex
corresponding to
\[
 (0,1,\ldots,1).
\]
After unfolding by symmetry, the continuum cube zero set is therefore
exactly the $k$ permutations of this vector.  Across the same historical
$32$-row snapshot this gives $107$ profiles in total; there is no additional
interior zero face.

Give these $k$ exceptional types arbitrary masses
$a_1\varepsilon,\ldots,a_k\varepsilon$, where every $a_i\geq0$.  Allow an
arbitrary self-cell value for each type and an arbitrary mutual-cell value
for every pair.  The coefficient of $\varepsilon^2$ is a quadratic form
\begin{equation}
 \boldsymbol a^{\mathsf T}B\boldsymbol a.
 \label{eq:full-profile-copositive}
\end{equation}

The coefficients are computed without finite-$\varepsilon$ sampling.  A map
of $H$ contributing to second order uses zero, one, or two exceptional
vertices.  Proper bulk colourings give the zero-exception term; the rooted
polynomials from Proposition~\ref{prop:needle} give the one-exception term;
and proper colourings of $H-\{u,v\}$ give the ordered two-exception term,
including the mutual exceptional-cell value when $uv\in E(H)$.  Subtracting
the exact first two derivatives of the chromatic target yields
$B$.  Dependence on every exceptional--exceptional cell value is affine, so
it is enough to test the vertices of that cell-value cube.

There are $21$ rows with $k=3$ and $11$ with $k=4$.  Thus the full audit has
\[
 21\cdot2^6+11\cdot2^{10}=12608
\]
exact matrices $B$.  Copositivity is checked on every coordinate face of the
mass simplex.  At an interior stationary point of a face $S$, exact linear
algebra gives
\[
 B_S\boldsymbol a=t\boldsymbol1,
 \qquad \boldsymbol1^{\mathsf T}\boldsymbol a=1,
\]
and the quadratic value is $t$.  No system with positive masses has $t<0$.
The $441$ singular augmented systems are all exactly inconsistent, rather
than omitted.  Hence all $12608$ matrices are copositive and this complete
second-order zero-profile test gives no new negative row.

The same stationary-point enumeration also identifies every zero of these
quadratic forms on the mass simplex.  There are $6304$ zero stationary
faces, and every one is the singleton profile $(0,1,\ldots,1)$ with zero
internal self-cell.  This merely splits one Tur\'an part into two
indistinguishable measurable pieces.  There is no other flat mass-cone
direction hidden by the word ``copositive.''

This also covers measurable exceptional-to-bulk kernels in a fixed zero
direction.  A $[0,1]$-valued function with conditional mean zero or one is
respectively zero or one almost everywhere, so the Boolean zero profiles
force the relevant bulk cells themselves.  Only the averaged internal cells
remain, and their affine dependence was tested above.

This is a genuine higher-order no-hit result, but it still does not prove a
cut-distance neighbourhood of local minimality.  An
$\varepsilon$-dependent profile may approach the finite zero set while its
small positive linear coefficient competes with the quadratic term.  The
exact zero mass-cone directions at the first interior point are only
Tur\'an refinements, and multiple coarse profiles at the threshold are now
coupled with one common exact density repair.  What remains is uniform
control of profiles depending on $\varepsilon$ and approaching the interior
zero set, and the analogous threshold mixture problem for arbitrary
exceptional-to-bulk fibres.  The negative Atlas $152$ mixed kernel above
shows that the latter cannot be closed by pointwise kernel nonnegativity.

\section{High density: Tur\'an versus the constant graphon}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

Put $p=1-1/x$.  The constant graphon has density
\[
 t(H,p)=p^m=\frac{(x-1)^m}{x^m},
\]
whereas the target is $\chi_H(x)/x^n$.  Hence the constant graphon is a
strict counterexample exactly when
\begin{equation}
 R_H(x):=x^{m-n}\chi_H(x)-(x-1)^m>0.
 \label{eq:R}
\end{equation}

The exact test gives a strong no-hit result for the baseline rows.

\begin{proposition}[Leading high-density coefficient]\label{prop:girth-leading}
Let $g$ be the girth of $H$ and let $N_g(H)$ be its number of (unoriented)
$g$-cycles.  Then
\[
 R_H(x)=(-1)^gN_g(H)x^{m-g+1}+O(x^{m-g}).
\]
Consequently the constant graphon is an asymptotic counterexample whenever
$g$ is even, while for odd $g$ it lies on the non-counterexample side for
all sufficiently large $x$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Whitney's rank expansion gives
\[
 x^{m-n}\chi_H(x)
 =\sum_{A\subseteq E(H)}(-1)^{|A|}x^{m-r(A)}.
\]
For every $j<g$, each $j$-edge subset is a forest and has rank $j$; these
terms cancel the corresponding binomial terms in $(x-1)^m$.  At rank
$g-1$, all $(g-1)$-edge subsets are still forests, but there are in addition
exactly the $N_g(H)$ edge sets of the shortest cycles, each of size $g$.
Their combined contribution is $(-1)^gN_g(H)x^{m-g+1}$.  All remaining
terms have smaller degree.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Constant comparison for all baseline open rows]
For every one of the $43$ tested rows and every real
$x\geq\chi(H)-1$,
\[
 R_H(x)<0.
\]
Thus the constant graphon has strictly larger $H$-density than the target
throughout the full required interval and cannot be a negative witness for
any of these rows.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For every tested graph, exact expansion gives
\[
 R_H(y+\chi(H)-1)=\sum_{j=0}^{d}r_jy^j
\]
with every $r_j<0$.  There are $24$ distinct coefficient vectors among the
$43$ graphs.  Therefore the polynomial is strictly negative for every
$y\geq0$.  The script accompanying this note computes the chromatic
polynomials independently and checks every coefficient as an integer.  It
also verifies Proposition~\ref{prop:girth-leading} graph by graph: $42$ rows
have girth three, and Atlas $104$ has girth five.  Thus their asymptotic
leading signs are negative before the stronger full-interval coefficient
test is applied.
\end{proof}

In the current catalogue, the historical set has been reduced to the
following $29$ open rows:
\[
\begin{gathered}

145,147,148,151,152,153,157,160,168,169,171,174,178,181,185,\\
188,194,196,199,203.
\end{gathered}
\]
The script reruns the strict coefficient test on exactly this current set;
all $29$ shifted polynomials are coefficientwise strictly negative, in $20$
distinct coefficient classes.  Thus the conclusion is independent of the
historical status of rows subsequently resolved by other arguments and the
three local witnesses in this note.

This explains why the even-girth method does not directly produce further
witnesses here.  Its conceptual form can be seen by writing
\[
 W=1-qU,
 \qquad
 \int U=1,
 \qquad q=1-p.
\]
For $A\subseteq E(H)$, let $r(A)$ be its graphic-matroid rank and
$\beta(A)=|A|-r(A)$.  Inclusion--exclusion for $t(H,W)$ and Whitney's
formula for the chromatic polynomial give the exact identity
\begin{equation}
 \Gamma_H(W)
 =\sum_{A\subseteq E(H)}(-1)^{|A|}q^{r(A)}
 \left(q^{\beta(A)}t((V(H),A),U)-1\right).
 \label{eq:cycle-rank}
\end{equation}
If $U$ is regular of degree one, every forest term vanishes.  The first
nonzero contribution is therefore controlled by the first cyclic matroid
rank.  The normalized complement of $T_k$ makes every parenthesis in
\eqref{eq:cycle-rank} zero.  For the constant graphon, $U=1$, and the first
cycle has sign determined by its parity.  This recovers the even-girth
obstruction and also explains the opposite sign for the triangle-containing
rows considered here.

The two endpoint comparison also extends to every bounded normalized
complement direction.  The complete quadratic coefficient in
\eqref{eq:cycle-rank} is
\[
 P_2(H)\bigl(t(P_3,U)-1\bigr)+N_3(H),
\]
where $P_2(H)=\sum_v\binom{d_H(v)}2$ and $N_3(H)$ is the triangle count.
Cauchy's inequality gives $t(P_3,U)\geq1$.  Every row in the 29-row audit
snapshot has a
triangle, so this coefficient is strict.  More precisely, if $0\leq U\leq
M$ for a fixed $M$, the explicit remainder estimate in
\path{notes/bounded_complement_high_density_stability.tex} gives
\[
 \Gamma_H(1-qU)\geq q^2\bigl(N_3(H)-qC_H(M)\bigr)>0
\]
for an explicit interval $0<q<N_3(H)/C_H(M)$.  Thus all $29$ snapshot rows,
and in particular all $28$ current open rows, pass not only the constant
endpoint but every fixed bounded complement shape.  The Tur\'an complements
have norm $1/q$ and therefore lie exactly
outside this fixed-$M$ regime.

The simplest such singular shape can also be excluded on the entire
admissible interval.  If $W_s=1-\mathbf 1_{S\times S}$ with $\mu(S)=s$,
then a homomorphism survives exactly when the vertices mapped into $S$ form
an independent set.  Thus
\[
 t(H,W_s)=\sum_{I\in\mathcal I(H)}s^{|I|}(1-s)^{v(H)-|I|}.
\]
For every row in that snapshot, exact factorization and strictly positive
Bernstein coefficients prove
$\Gamma_H(W_s)=s^3(1-s)^\ell R_H(s)>0$ for every nonzero $s$ in the full
required interval.  The $29$ certificates, in $25$ coefficient classes,
are listed independently in
\path{notes/singular_clique_complement_high_density_test.tex}.  Hence a
single concentrating deleted clique is also a no-hit direction.  The same
note uses the exact cluster-rank expansion to prove that $r$ deleted diagonal
cliques are uniformly positive whenever $rq<\alpha_H$ for an explicit
constant: their leading term is
\[
 (P_2(H)-N_3(H))\left(\sum_{i=1}^r s_i^3-q^2\right),
 \qquad q=\sum_{i=1}^r s_i^2,
\]
and power mean makes the first sum at least $q^{3/2}/\sqrt r$, while the
remaining graphic-rank terms are $O_H(q^2)$.  Every snapshot row has
$P_2(H)-N_3(H)>0$.  This covers every $r=o(1/q)$ family.  Thus only the
critical $r\asymp1/q$ scale or a non-clique/moving singular complement
would remain under that estimate alone.

An entire canonical nonregular family is also excluded on the full interval.
If $W$ is a finite or countable union of clique blocks of masses $s_i$,
possibly together with a leftover dust set on which $W$ is zero, then,
because every open $H$ is connected,
\[
 t(H,W)=\sum_i s_i^{v(H)}.
\]
If $m=\max_i s_i$, then
$p=\sum_i s_i^2\leq m^2+(1-m)^2$ and
$t(H,W)\geq m^{v(H)}$.  Exact bivariate Bernstein certificates compare this
lower bound with $\Phi_H(p)$ simultaneously over every feasible pair
$(m,p)$, in $20$ coefficient classes.  They are nonnegative on the complete
required intervals for all $29$ rows, with equality only at $p=1$.  Thus no
finite or countable clique-union-with-dust graphon supplies a counterexample.
A union of two cliques, whose complement is a complete bipartite cut, is
included.  One clique plus dust is the canonical persistent-hub complement:
the complement equals one on every pair incident to the dust set.

The companion note removes the part-count restriction by propagating the
single power-sum defect
\[
 D=\sum_i s_i^3-q^2.
\]
If $m=\max_i s_i$, every higher component power-sum defect is at most an
explicit combinatorial coefficient times $mD$.  The exact cluster-rank
identity consequently yields
\[
 \Gamma_H(W)\geq(c_H-\Lambda_H\sqrt q)D,
 \qquad c_H=P_2(H)-N_3(H)>0.
\]
This proves nonnegativity sufficiently near $p=1$ for every finite or
countable complete-multipartite graphon, with no restriction on the number
or imbalance of its parts.  Equality is exactly the balanced Turan family.
All $29$ snapshot rows pass, with exact $\Lambda_H$ values listed in the
companion note.  Thus only a non-clique or otherwise moving singular
complement remains possible within the high-density methodology.

Even there, the complete graphic-rank-two contribution is universally
nonnegative.  For $V=1-W$, $q=\int V$,
\[
 (P_2(H)-N_3(H))(t(P_3,V)-q^2)
 +N_3(H)(t(P_3,V)-t(K_3,V))\geq0.
\]
The two defects are respectively degree variance and failure of transitivity.
Their common zero set is exactly a union of equal clique blocks, hence a
balanced Turan complement.  Both coefficients are strict on all $29$
snapshot rows.  The unresolved high-density step is therefore a quantitative
stability theorem showing that simultaneous smallness of these defects
controls every higher-rank term.

The entire exactly regular slice of that stability problem can also be
closed.  If the arbitrary measurable complement $V$ has constant degree
$q$, let
\[
 D_V=q^2-t(K_3,V).
\]
The normalized self-adjoint operator $T_V/q$ is a contraction.  Its spectrum
proves that $D_V$ controls every cycle defect; a spanning-tree telescoping
argument then controls every connected cyclic subgraph.  The independent
theorem in \path{notes/stochastic_block_high_density_stability.tex} gives
the uniform bound
\[
 \Gamma_H(1-V)\geq\bigl(N_3(H)-\Xi_Hq\bigr)D_V.
\]
All $29$ open rows have $N_3(H)>0$ and exact finite $\Xi_H$, so every regular
complement graphon is strictly positive for sufficiently small $q$, except
at the balanced clique-block complements, where it is the exact Turan
equality.  No equal-cell, finite-step, or discrete-density assumption is
used.  A symmetric doubly stochastic $r$-cell matrix is the special case
$q=1/r$ and includes every degree-preserving coarse perturbation of $T_r$.
Nonregular complements remain outside this spectral theorem, but the
independent maximum-degree theorem closes a broad nonregular neighbourhood.
For an arbitrary complement put
\[
 X_V=t(P_3,V)-q^2,
 \qquad Y_V=t(P_3,V)-t(K_3,V),
 \qquad \Delta=\lVert d_V\rVert_\infty.
\]
The tree-indexed Markov representation, H\"older, and relative entropy give
the sharp comparison
\[
 q^{e(T)}\leq t(T,V)\leq\int d_V^{e(T)}
\]
for every tree.  A pointwise path telescoping reduces every failed long-cycle
closure to at most its length minus two triangle failures.  Consequently the
graphic-rank expansion satisfies
\[
 \Gamma_H(1-V)\geq
 (c_H-\Lambda_H\Delta)X_V
 +(N_3(H)-\Omega_H\Delta)Y_V.
\]
The exact constants and proof are in the independent note
{\small\path{notes/maximum_degree_complement_high_density_stability.tex}}.
All $29$ open rows have an explicit positive radius
\[
 \Delta<\min\{c_H/\Lambda_H,N_3(H)/\Omega_H\};
\]
the radii range from $7/8079$ to $8/161$.  Thus neither regularity nor a
finite-step representation is required.  By itself this theorem leaves only
families retaining vertices of complement degree bounded away from zero while
the average complement density tends to zero; the later high-degree-set
decomposition closes that residual regime as well.

The complementary persistent-hub regime also admits a full finite-dimensional
theorem.  Split off an exceptional set of mass $s$ and put
\[
 W_{BB}=1,
 \qquad W_{AB}=1-a,
 \qquad W_{AA}=1-r,
 \qquad0\leq a,r\leq1.

maximum-degree theorem gives no information when $a$ stays positive.  The

open rows.  Hence the entire cube $0\leq s\leq1/3$, $0\leq a,r\leq1$ is
nonnegative.  This simultaneously contains the one-clique hole, one clique
plus dust, and every constant two-block interpolation between their singular
shapes.  The next theorem removes the remaining fibre restriction near the
high-density endpoint.

Nonconstant fibres with genuinely persistent cross defect are also excluded.
Suppose $W=1$ on the square of the core $B$, while both $W|_{A\times B}$ and

 a(z)=\int_B(1-W(z,y))\,d\mu_B(y),
 \qquad E_2=\int_Aa(z)^2\,d\mu_A(z),

\[
 2e(H)u-v(H)+\sum_x(1-u)^{\deg_H(x)}
 \geq(2e(H)-v(H))u^2,

 \Gamma_H(W)\geq
 s(2e(H)-v(H))E_2-K_Hs^2.
\]
The exact theorem and constants are in

 \Gamma_H(W)\geq
 s(1-s)^{v(H)-1}(2e(H)-v(H))E_2-C_Hq^2.
\]
If $A^2$ is also complete, then
$q^2\leq4s^2(1-s)^2E_2$, and the whole energy range, including

Finally, the union-bound remainder is superadditive between the cut and
internal defect-edge families.  Apply the maximum-degree theorem to the
internal-only graphon, whose complement degree is at most $s$.  Its variance

$s^3\bar r\sqrt{E_2}$ by Young's inequality.  This proves nonnegativity for
