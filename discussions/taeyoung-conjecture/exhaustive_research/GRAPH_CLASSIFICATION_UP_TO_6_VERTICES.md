# Classification of the homomorphism lower bound for graphs on at most six vertices

We consider every unlabeled graph $H$ on at most six vertices that is non-bipartite, has no isolated vertices, and has no isolated-edge component. There are exactly **117** such graphs. The question is whether, for every graphon $W$ of edge density $p=t(K_2,W)$,

$$
t(H,W)\ge \Phi_H(p):=(1-p)^{v(H)}\chi_H\!\left(\frac1{1-p}\right)
$$

whenever $p\ge 1-1/(\chi(H)-1)$.

Current totals are **74 positive**, **23 negative**, and **20 open**. The preserved baseline for this research pass is 66 positive, 23 negative, and 28 open; the eight changes since then are complete positive proofs for Atlas 126, 127, 137, 139, 145, 148, 160, and 178, and the negative set is unchanged.

## Reading the table

Each row is one graph, drawn in the **Graph** cell. **Atlas ID** and **graph6** identify it exactly and machine-readably.

**Status** is the mathematical classification.

* 🟢 **Positive** — the inequality is proved for this $H$. The Reason column names the accepted proof.
* 🔴 **Negative** — the displayed graphon is a strict counterexample, checked in exact rational arithmetic.
* ⚪ **Open** — no accepted proof and no exact counterexample. This records the present state of the search only; it is not evidence that the inequality is true.

**Lean** is the machine-checked state of the row in [lean/](lean/), which is independent of the mathematical status.

* ✅ **Verified** — the row's Lean proposition is proved with no `sorry`, and `#print axioms` reports only `propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound`. That proposition is `SatisfiesLowerBound` for a positive row and `ViolatesLowerBound` for a negative one. **95 rows.**
* 🔧 **Believed** — the mathematical status is accepted, but the Lean bridge for it is still a `sorry`. **1 row** (Atlas 126).
* ⚪ **Unresolved** — Lean asserts only `P ∨ ¬P`. **21 rows**: the 20 mathematically open rows, plus Atlas 127, whose computer-assisted certificate is accepted here but is not yet carried into `lean/`.

**Reason or exact certificate** gives the accepted justification. Every method named there is catalogued [after the table](#positive-methods).

## What a ✅ means

A ✅ reports what the Lean kernel has checked, not how strong the mathematical evidence is. A 🔧 or ⚪ row is not thereby in doubt mathematically, and a ✅ row carries no extra mathematical weight.

The Lean proof of a ✅ row may deviate from the route named in the Reason column, which records the accepted mathematical justification rather than the formalized one. Two rows differ: Atlas 7 ($K_3$) and Atlas 106 ($K_3\sqcup K_3$) are listed under the odd-cycle bound and component multiplicativity, but Lean discharges both by the pure-chordal theorem, since both are pure chordal with every maximal clique of size 3, and that theorem's certificate factors over connected components, so it needs no connectivity hypothesis.

## Positive methods

Thirty-two arguments account for the 74 positive rows. **The general universal-vertex lifting claim is not used anywhere in this classification**: the table does not assume that positivity is preserved by an arbitrary join, or even by adjoining a universal vertex to an arbitrary positive graph.

### Base theorems and closure operations

**Odd-cycle theorem** — Atlas 7, 38. [notes/paper_new_region2_v3.tex](notes/paper_new_region2_v3.tex) proves the Goodman-style lower bound for odd cycles. This is the base case that most of the rooted attachment theorems below extend.

**Independent paw proof** — Atlas 15. A self-contained argument for the paw, with no separate note; it is the base of the paw-cone family.

**Pure-chordal theorem** — Atlas 17, 18, 42, 46, 47, 51, 52, 144, 150, 161, 162, 163, 164, 167, 195, 201, 202, 207, 208 (19 rows, the single largest family). [notes/pure_chordal.tex](notes/pure_chordal.tex) gives a graphon-direct proof for chordal graphs whose maximal cliques all have one common size, using only finite-product integration, an elementary Gibbs inequality, the regularization $W_\varepsilon=\varepsilon+(1-\varepsilon)W$, a graphon proof of the Moon--Moser recurrence, and finite clique-tree combinatorics. It needs no finite-graph approximation, regularity lemma, cut metric, or degree hypothesis, and its certificate factors over connected components.

**Concrete clique–odd-cycle theorem** — Atlas 187. [notes/clique_oddcycle_join.tex](notes/clique_oddcycle_join.tex) states a universal-vertex lifting theorem; only the concrete clique-joined-to-odd-cycle instance is used here, not the general claim.

**Cone-over-forest theorem** — Atlas 40, 111, 117, 135, 136. [notes/forest_cone_graphon_bound.tex](notes/forest_cone_graphon_bound.tex) proves the bound for a cone over a forest, indexed in the table by component and edge count.

**Multiplicativity over connected components** — Atlas 84, 106. The bound multiplies over components once every component is itself covered.

**Whiskering** — Atlas 94. [notes/whiskering.tex](notes/whiskering.tex) proves closure under attaching a pendant leaf at every vertex; Atlas 94 is the whiskering of the covered Atlas 7.

## Negative certificates

**Tensor counterexamples** — Atlas 48, 50, 129, 140, 141, 143, 149, 158, 159, 170, 173, 182, 184, 186, 189, 190, 197, 198, 204 (19 rows). The tensor graphon $T_{k_1} \otimes \cdots \otimes T_{k_s}$ has

$$
p=\prod_i\left(1-\frac1{k_i}\right),\qquad
t(H,W)=\prod_i\frac{\chi_H(k_i)}{k_i^{v(H)}},
$$

so a violation is a finite rational computation. The search exhaustively checked one- and two-factor products with factors at most 500, repeated and consecutive products with endpoints at most 1000, and two million arbitrary products of lengths 2–24 with factors at most 5000. Every displayed certificate was then re-verified in exact rational arithmetic. Failure to find a tensor counterexample is not a proof of positivity. Relatedly, [notes/even_girth_false.tex](notes/even_girth_false.tex) shows that even girth alone already yields infinitely many tensor–Turán violations.

**Turán-local counterexamples** — Atlas 166, 172, 206 (3 rows), from [notes/turan_local_and_high_density_negative_tests.tex](notes/turan_local_and_high_density_negative_tests.tex). Atlas 166 and 172 arise from equal two-microclass realizations of negative measurable-kernel Hessian modes at $T_2$; Atlas 206 arises from a one-diagonal first-variation perturbation at $T_3$. All three use $\varepsilon=1/4$ and no tensor search.

**Atlas 152 fractional-fibre counterexample** — [notes/atlas_152_fractional_fibre_local_counterexample.tex](notes/atlas_152_fractional_fibre_local_counterexample.tex), a five-step perturbation of $T_2$. The threshold analysis first eliminated broad positive subcones: common-fibre consistency proves every mixture of at most two indicator profiles nonnegative by six exact degree-eight determinant certificates; separate factorizations settle all axis mixtures; nine face-to-face-audited degree-four certificates settle coordinatewise chains; four more settle constant-displacement lines; Jensen gives explicit low-dispersion radii; and the comonotone theorem settles nested indicators. The counterexample then lies exactly in the sharply predicted residual cone — its two mean profiles are incomparable and have unequal displacement, one is strict-interior, and its corresponding fibre is genuinely fractional. With complementary indicator fibres in the first bulk part and constant fibres $1/16,1$ in the second, the quadratic matrix has determinant $-2943/312500000000$, and the mass vector $(1/10,9/10)$ gives $-8631/2000000000$. The standalone proof evaluates the full graphon at $\varepsilon=1/3000$, so the classification does not rest on an asymptotic truncation.

All four local constructions above sit near balanced Turán graphons in the broad analytic sense, but Atlas 152 is not counted as a member of the ordinary Turán-local catalogue family.

**Repaired-needle fibre analysis (historical)** — Atlas 137, 139, 145, 148. The exact repaired needle is a positive multiple of $u^2v^2$, so only the two profile axes remain first-order flat. The full arbitrary-fibre interaction on those axes is $\kappa_H(1-x)(1-y)$, independently of whether the profiles lie on the same or opposite axes, of their feasible fibre overlap, and of the mutual exceptional-cell value, so every probability mixture in the complete residual axis cone has a nonnegative quadratic coefficient.
